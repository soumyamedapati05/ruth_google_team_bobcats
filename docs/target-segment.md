# Who to Target First: Beachhead Segment for an NYC AI Scholarship and Opportunity Finder

*Research memo, prepared 2026-10-23 for the Ruth x Google "Code for Good" team. Research only. Every statistic is linked to its source and dated. Where a figure could not be found, the memo says so. Anything labeled "derived" is our own arithmetic on cited numbers, not a published figure.*

---

## TL;DR

**Primary beachhead:** **Low-income, first-generation 11th and 12th graders at Bronx public high schools.** Start with the South and Southwest Bronx (community school districts 7, 9, 10 and 12: Mott Haven, Highbridge/Grand Concourse, Fordham/Kingsbridge, East Tremont/Crotona). Reach them through peer-leader college-access programs (CARA), CUNY College Now, and the school counselors and CBO college advisors who already work there.

**Secondary segment:** **First- and second-year students at Bronx CUNY community colleges (Hostos, Bronx CC)**, especially students who plan to transfer. This is the same community one step later, and it shares the same counselors, CBOs and family networks.

**Why the Bronx**

- It is NYC's highest-need borough on almost every measure that matters for this product:
  - poverty: 28% of residents, and nearly 40% of children
  - student homelessness: 17.4% of students
  - lowest adult college attainment, a proxy for first-gen
  - highest share of households with no home broadband (36.7%) or cellular-only internet (20.3%), so a phone-first, "say a few sentences" app fits
- The dataset you already have fits this group almost exactly:
  - CUNY scholarships
  - HESC/TAP/Excelsior
  - CUNY College Now
  - SEO Scholars
  - campus open houses
  - free STEM programs

**Why juniors and seniors rather than all of 9th to 12th grade**

- Juniors and seniors feel the deadline pressure, can act on a match immediately, and are the group counselors and CBOs are measured on.
- Grades 9 and 10 stay in the product as the "almost eligible" pipeline and as a later expansion.
- It is also an honest wedge against Common App: New York now requires every senior to file a FAFSA or TAP application or sign a waiver, so *filing the FAFSA* is no longer the main gap. The gap is everything else: local and private scholarships, free programs that build eligibility, and the steps after a match.

**What undocumented and DACA students can and can't get**

- **Can get, through the NYS DREAM Act** ([HESC](https://hesc.ny.gov/applying-aid/nys-dream-act); [HESC FAQ](https://hesc.ny.gov/applying-aid/nys-dream-act/nys-dream-act-frequently-asked-questions)):
  - TAP
  - the Excelsior Scholarship
  - the Enhanced Tuition Award
  - the NYS STEM scholarship
  - other NYS-administered aid
- **DREAM Act eligibility requires:**
  - qualifying status (no lawful status, DACA, TPS, or U/T visa)
  - **plus one of:** graduating from a NYS high school after at least 2 years of attendance, a NYS high school equivalency, or in-state tuition at SUNY or CUNY
- **How to apply:** through HESC's separate DREAM Act portal, *not* the FAFSA. Applications for 2026-27 and 2027-28 are open. Students can start while still in high school.
- **Cannot get:** federal aid (Pell, federal loans, work-study). Undocumented students can't file the FAFSA for themselves (per HESC).
- **Private scholarships:** many require citizenship or an SSN. Some do not. For example, [TheDream.US](https://www.csi.cuny.edu/admissions/paying-college/dream-us) partners with CUNY campuses and covers DACA/TPS students and those meeting past DACA criteria. Its terms change every year, so verify them.

### 2g. FAFSA completion

- **New York's universal FAFSA law.** Starting with the Class of 2025, districts must make sure every senior files a FAFSA, a NYS DREAM Act/TAP application, or a signed opt-out waiver ([NCAN](https://ncan.org/Web/News/NY_Passes_Universal_FAFSA.aspx); [Governor, Apr 2024](https://www.governor.ny.gov/news/governor-hochul-announces-key-initiatives-support-students-and-increase-access-higher)).
- **Statewide completion rates** (the HESC dashboard data file [FAFSA_Completion_Data_Summary_September.xlsx](https://hesc.ny.gov/sites/default/files/2026-09/FAFSA_Completion_Data_Summary_September.xlsx), downloaded and parsed for this memo):
  - **Class of 2025:** 77.1% by Sept 30 2025
  - **Class of 2026:** **75.9% by Aug 31 2026, vs. NCAN's national 63.5%**
  - Method: cumulative completions divided by NYSED 12th-grade enrollment, per [HESC methodology](https://hesc.ny.gov/sites/default/files/2026-06/FAFSA%20Data%20and%20Methodology_LetterHead_06012026.pdf).
  - Note: a SUNY release cited 68% for the Class of 2025 ([SUNY, Jan 2026](https://www.suny.edu/suny-news/press-releases/1-26/1-23-26-2/)), probably measured differently or at a different date.
- **NYC counts.** The same HESC file shows about **60,600 completed FAFSAs from NYC public and charter high-school seniors in the Class of 2026** (Bronx ~12,400; Brooklyn ~16,800; Manhattan ~12,400; Queens ~15,100; Staten Island ~3,800). These are *derived* by summing school-level rows, with "<5" cells counted as 0.
- **No NYC rate.** We could **not compute a reliable NYC or borough completion rate**, because the file gives no per-school enrollment denominators. No published NYC-specific rate turned up.
- **Pre-mandate income gap.** At NY schools with the most low-income students, completion was **41.8%**, vs. **57.2%** at schools with the fewest ([Ed Trust-NY, mid-cycle](https://newyork.edtrust.org/adopting-universal-free-application-for-federal-student-aid-in-new-york)).
- **Implication for the pitch.** FAFSA filing is now largely mandated, so "help with the FAFSA" is not your wedge. Local and private scholarships, free eligibility-building programs, and post-match follow-through are.

## 4. Recommendation

### Primary beachhead
**First-generation, low-income 11th and 12th graders at public high schools in the South and Southwest Bronx (districts 7, 9, 10, 12), reached through counselors, college-access CBOs and peer leaders.**

**Why this scores highest on need × fit × reach**

1. **Largest unmet need**
   - The Bronx has the highest poverty of any borough (~28%; ~40% of children).
   - It has the highest student homelessness (17.4%). Four of its districts are above 1 in 6.
   - It has the lowest adult BA attainment (~20%), which means most students there are first-gen.
   - Citywide, more than 28% of graduates in recent cohorts did not enroll in college within a semester, and two-year college enrollment fell from ~22% to under 12%. Students at the margin are being lost.
2. **Best product fit**
   - Your current listings (CUNY scholarships, HESC aid, College Now, SEO Scholars, campus open houses, free STEM programs) are the right resources for this student.
   - Phone-first, "say a few sentences" input suits a borough where 1 in 5 households relies on cellular data only.
   - "Almost eligible" maps to concrete junior-year moves: take a free College Now course, join a free summer STEM program, attend a Lehman or Hostos open house.
3. **Easiest to reach**
   - The Bronx has a dense layer of intermediaries already measured on college access (Section 5).
   - The Bronx CUNY campuses (Hostos, Bronx CC, Lehman) host College Now and open houses. One campus partnership can give you both the HS students and the secondary segment.

**Concrete pitch line.** *"Our first users are first-gen juniors and seniors in the South Bronx, where about 1 in 6 students was homeless last year, more than a third of homes lack wired broadband, and a high-school counselor's caseload of ~190 students is shared with scheduling, crises and mental health."* Optionally add: *"…only about a fifth of a public-school counselor's time typically goes to college advising."* Attribute that to NACAC's national data.

### Secondary segment
**First- and second-year students at Bronx CUNY community colleges (Hostos, Bronx CC), especially transfer-bound students.**

- At Hostos, 84% come from households earning under $30k and 55% are first-gen. CUNY-wide, 60% are first-gen and 57% receive Pell.
- These students are your HS beachhead 12 to 24 months later. Serving both lets you show the "after Common App" continuity in your positioning.
- They can use the opportunities side right away: hackathons, company student events, internships, transfer scholarships.

### What we are deliberately *not* doing first
- **Undocumented students, students in shelter, and foster youth** are not the lead segments, even though their need is severe. They need trust, case management and strict privacy that a hackathon MVP can't yet guarantee.
- **They still benefit from day one, through features inside the beachhead:**
  - a "No SSN / citizenship required" filter
  - DREAM Act and TAP explainers
  - surfacing the unaccompanied-homeless-youth independent-student FAFSA rule
- **These features are flagged, not tracked.** The app never asks a student for their immigration or housing status.

## 6. Personas (composites, not real people)

> **All three personas are COMPOSITES** built from the data above. Names, ages and details are illustrative. Do not present them as real students.

### Persona 1: Yesenia, 16, junior, small public high school in Highbridge (District 9, Bronx)
- **Situation:** Dominican-American and first-gen. Her mom is a home health aide and her dad drives for a delivery app. Home internet is her phone's data plan. Her school's one counselor also handles programming and crises.
- **What she's tried:** Googled "scholarships for Hispanic students" and got national sweepstakes-style lists and sites asking for her email and phone. She went to one college fair. She doesn't know what "College Now" is.
- **What she needs:** things she can do *this semester* that are free, near her, and make her a stronger applicant, plus local money she actually has a shot at.
- **What the app shows her** (from your dataset types):
  - CUNY College Now course at Hostos or Lehman
  - a free spring or summer STEM program
  - an open house at Lehman
  - a plain-language explanation that TAP and Excelsior could cover CUNY tuition if her family qualifies
  - "almost eligible": "Complete one College Now course to qualify for X," or "this hackathon counts as STEM experience for Y"

### Persona 2: Malik, 17, senior, Mott Haven (District 7, Bronx)
- **Situation:** He moved in with his aunt after his family lost their apartment. He is "doubled up", like 53% of NYC's homeless students. He works weekends. His attendance slipped junior year.
- **What he's tried:** He started the FAFSA because his school requires it, then got stuck on parent information.
- **What he needs:**
  - to learn that, as an unaccompanied homeless youth, he may qualify as an **independent student** on the FAFSA once the school's McKinney-Vento liaison confirms his status
  - local scholarships with near deadlines and no essays longer than 500 words
  - CUNY options
- **What the app shows him:**
  - a "talk to your school's liaison" step (no data stored about his housing)
  - CUNY scholarships ranked by realistic odds
  - free test-prep or college workshops near him
  - a reminder cadence by SMS or phone

### Persona 3: Daniela, 19, first-year at Hostos Community College (South Bronx)
- **Situation:** She came from Ecuador at 14 and graduated from a NYC public high school. She is undocumented without DACA. Spanish is her first language. She works 25 hours a week.
- **What she's tried:** She was told she "can't get financial aid" and paid her first semester partly out of pocket.
- **What she needs:**
  - to learn that the **NYS DREAM Act** may open TAP and Excelsior to her: she has a NYS diploma after 2+ years of high school
  - scholarships that **don't require an SSN or citizenship**
  - paid or free opportunities that build toward a transfer
- **What the app shows her:**
  - a DREAM Act explainer and link to HESC
  - CUNY scholarships open regardless of status
  - TheDream.US as a possible option (flagged "check eligibility")
  - free CUNY hackathons and career events
- **Privacy:** she never has to tell the app her immigration status. She just toggles a "show only no-SSN-required" filter, which isn't saved.

### Pathos opening (honest, not exploitative)
> *"Picture a junior in the South Bronx (we'll call her Yesenia; she's a composite of students we read about, not one person). She's doing everything right: good grades, a weekend job, helping her younger brother with homework. Her school's counselor is responsible for nearly two hundred students and also handles schedules, crises and mental health. Her family has never been through college admissions. Most of her internet comes through her phone. When she searches 'scholarships,' she gets national contests with ten thousand applicants and sites that want her phone number. Ten minutes from her school there are free college courses, free summer STEM programs and local scholarships she'd have a real shot at, and nobody has connected her to them. We built [app] so that a student like Yesenia can say three sentences about herself and see, in her own language and on her own phone, what she qualifies for now, and what she's one step away from."*

**Why it's honest:**
- It is framed as a composite.
- It uses only cited facts: the ~1:190 HS counselor ratio, the Bronx cellular-only rate, College Now.
- It avoids trauma details.
- It promises matching, not money.

## 7. Risks and sensitivities

1. **Privacy for undocumented and mixed-status students**
   - Don't collect immigration status, SSNs, home addresses or housing status. Use **ephemeral filters** ("show no-SSN-required") instead of stored profile fields. Don't log raw free-text "about me" inputs longer than needed. Say plainly on screen what is stored.
   - Federal law limits FAFSA data use, but advocates say families are still afraid. In June 2026, members of Congress introduced the Protecting Student Privacy Act to bar immigration use of FAFSA data. California saw a ~42% drop in first-time mixed-status applicants to its state dream act application in one cycle ([Rep. Randall press release](https://randall.house.gov/media/press-releases/randall-casar-norton-introduce-bill-prevent-fafsa-data-being-used-immigration); [KQED](https://www.kqed.org/news/12083915/amid-immigration-crackdown-officials-worry-about-decline-in-california-dream-act-applications); [NCAN mixed-status guidance](https://ncan.org/Web/News/Updated-FAFSA-Guidance-for-Mixed-Status-Families.aspx)).
   - "No data selling" is necessary but not enough. Also commit to **no data sharing with government or third parties, data minimization, and easy deletion**.
   - Note that the Gemini API (AI Studio) terms may allow Google to use inputs on unpaid tiers. Check the current terms, and use a paid tier or strip personal details before sending prompts.
2. **Over-promising**
   - "Ranked by realistic odds" must not read as a guarantee. Show *why* (eligibility criteria met) and the date each listing was last verified.
   - Avoid statements like "you will get $X." Don't call students "eligible" for need-based aid without income data. Say "may qualify".
   - AI hallucination risk: generate the "why you match" line **only from structured listing fields**, never from model memory. Stale or expired listings are the fastest way to lose counselor trust.
3. **Language access**
   - Prioritize **Spanish** first: ~67% of NYC ELLs. Then Chinese, Arabic, Bengali, Russian and Haitian Creole (top ELL languages). Uzbek, French and Ukrainian are growing among newcomers.
   - City agencies use the Local Law 30 list (Spanish, Chinese, Russian, Bengali, Haitian Creole, Korean, Arabic, Urdu, French, Polish).
   - Gemini can take input and respond in these languages, but most listings are in English. Translate summaries and keep the original text linked.
4. **Phone-only access**
   - Design mobile-first, light pages, and voice input. Consider SMS or WhatsApp reminders.
   - Don't require an email login to see results. Many teens use school email that they lose at graduation.
5. **Students in crisis**
   - For students in shelter or doubled up, the app can point to the school's McKinney-Vento liaison and AFC resources. It must not try to determine homelessness itself.
6. **Counselor and CBO trust**
   - Intermediaries will only push the app if it saves them time and is accurate. Give Scouts verification badges and a "last verified" date. Don't make it look like a replacement for counselors.
7. **Data gaps to acknowledge in Q&A**
   - no current borough-level college-enrollment rate
   - no NYC-specific FAFSA completion rate (only counts)
   - no CUNY-wide first-gen figure after 2022
   - no borough-level counselor ratios
   - undocumented student counts are dated estimates

## 8. Biggest uncertainties
- **The size of the "can't find local scholarships" problem is assumed, not measured.** We found no NYC survey of how many first-gen students miss local or private scholarships. Validate it with 10-15 quick interviews of Bronx juniors and counselors before the pitch. That makes a strong slide.
- **Counselor capacity by borough is unknown.** The 1:190 high-school ratio is citywide and self-reported. The "~1 college-advising FTE per ~950 students" figure is our derivation from a national time-use number.
- **FAFSA mandate effects.** New York's universal FAFSA law appears to have pushed completion to ~76% statewide (vs. 63.5% nationally), but NYC and borough rates couldn't be computed. If Bronx completion still lags, FAFSA help could be a secondary hook.
- **ELL counts differ by source and year** (~136k vs. ~174k). Cite the source each time.
- **Partner capacity and willingness** (CARA, College Now offices, Options) is unverified. These are candidates, not commitments.
